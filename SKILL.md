# Evaluating Job Offers

## Overview
Compare **decision-relevant value**, not recruiter headline total compensation. Separate verified facts from assumptions, Guaranteed compensation from contingent upside, spendable cash from restricted wealth, and individual economics from household economics.

Prioritize mainland China campus technical hiring (including robotics/algorithm roles) when that is the user's context. Retain other-region routes: determine work location, tax residency, currency and year before applying local rules. Preserve five lenses: **cash/assets, living costs, time, growth, and family**; company size or a weighted score cannot override a hard constraint.

## Screen before scoring
1. Reuse stated priorities, exclusions, deadlines and previously supplied facts. Mark each option `eligible`, `excluded`, or `pending verification` against hard limits such as location, maximum travel, minimum dependable income or work authorization. Unknown is not a pass; exclude only a confirmed violation.
2. Distinguish **intent (意向)**, **conversion internship (转正实习)**, **conditional offer (附条件 offer)** and **formal employment offer (正式录用)**. Record the actual written conditions and remaining approvals; a title alone proves neither legal effect nor certainty. For conversion, verify funded headcount, criteria, decision date and fallback. Model internship and conversion/failure paths separately; do not book post-conversion salary as guaranteed.
3. Provide a provisional, conditional recommendation using known facts. Ask **at most 1–3 high-impact questions per turn** (zero if enough is known; honor a lower user limit). Each question targets one decision-changing uncertainty, not a disguised questionnaire. Reuse answers; do not repeat declined/private questions. Offer ranges or opt-out household analysis without names, addresses or intimate details. Put other unknowns in an assumptions register, not an unsolicited question bank.

## Evidence rules
- **Do not fabricate** compensation, tax, benefit, work-hour, location, or career facts.
- For current tax/social-benefit rules, housing costs, company practices, or historical offer bands, research the relevant **jurisdiction** and date. If browsing is unavailable or declined, leave current facts unverified and use labeled scenarios. Label evidence as `Offer`, `Official`, `Employee report`, `Historical`, or `Assumption`, with date and scope.
- Match evidence to the question: written terms for guarantees, current official rules for tax, same-team reports for actual hours/payout, and manager/project evidence for duties and resources. An official recruiting slogan does not establish daily practice.
- Prefer ranges to false precision. When sources conflict, show the conflict and use a conservative base case.
- Ask only for missing information that can materially change the result.

## Analysis flow
1. **Normalize compensation.** Declare a common period: joining calendar year, first complete 12 months, or steady year. Show partial-year pay, probation, eligibility and one-off/clawback cash separately. Split each offer into Guaranteed, target, and upside cases. Treat performance bonus, equity, sign-on, retention, relocation, pensions, employer matches, housing funds, and other benefits separately.
2. **Build a risk-adjusted case.** Discount uncertain bonus/equity using evidence or explicit scenarios; never silently count headline "N months" as guaranteed.
3. **Estimate after-tax cash and wealth.** Apply current local tax, social insurance/payroll deductions, and employee contributions. Keep employer-funded or restricted assets separate from spendable cash. Reconcile each item once: an included subsidy, reimbursed expense or sold equity proceeds must not be counted again as extra income/assets.
4. **Model living costs.** Use comparable housing, utilities, food, transport, relocation, and commute assumptions.
5. **Use Household mode when relevant.** Model shared rent, duplicated housing, partner location, childcare/caregiving, cross-city travel, and combined commute. Savings in after-tax expenses are not equivalent to the same amount of pre-tax salary.
6. **Measure time value.** Estimate weekly hours, commute, PTO, on-call/travel, then show effective hourly compensation or annual discretionary time when useful. Count only additional hours; if weekly hours include on-call or working weeks already deduct leave, do not add/deduct them twice. Preserve leave feasibility and discretionary time as options for rest, study, interviewing and caregiving.
7. **Assess career option value.** Compare role scope, learning, manager/team, promotion path, skill scarcity, brand, future mobility, and downside risk. For robotics/algorithms, verify actual research/training vs integration, testing, deployment and customer-site duties; mentor availability/review cadence; usable equipment, lawful data access and compute; delivery ownership and site/travel limits. A job title or company scale is insufficient. A small firm with a good mentor can win on growth if resources and business continuity are credible. Select the few unresolved checks that can change the decision. Keep subjective scores separate from financial calculations.
8. **Run sensitivity and multi-year paths.** Show what changes the decision: bonus payout, rent, tax treatment, commute, partner location, hours, equity value, conversion or promotion assumptions. Compare first-year and steady-year cash, 3–4-year individual/household cumulative surplus, and downside/exit paths without inventing conversion probabilities or guaranteed raises.

## Output contract
Lead with the provisional recommendation and its conditions, then the hard-constraint/status screen. Cover (1) evidence/assumptions, (2) normalized compensation with period, (3) after-tax cash and wealth, (4) living/Household mode and cumulative surplus, (5) time/WLB/leave options, (6) career growth, (7) sensitivity or gross-salary break-even, and (8) the remaining 0–3 decision-critical questions. Combine sections for short requests and label unknowns rather than forcing empty tables. Give a recommendation only from the user's stated priorities; otherwise describe the trade-off frontier. State which specific new evidence would reverse the recommendation.
